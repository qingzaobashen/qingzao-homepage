## Which Life Hacks Feel Like Secretly Striking It Rich Once You Learn Them?

Lately I've been enjoying a particular kind of thrill: knowing a life hack your friends don't know yet.

On the surface, nothing changes. In your head, you're already tallying the win: this round, pure profit.

Here are a few that have given me that "illicit wealth" feeling.

---

First, supermarkets.

Supermarket price tags carry a secret code. On many chain-store tags there's a small letter in the bottom-right corner: A is regular price, C is clearance, S is a special offer. The best one is D. D stands for Discontinued: the item is being phased out and needs to sell fast.

The first time I learned this, I stood in front of the snack aisle feeling less like a shopper and more like someone decoding enemy intelligence.

Holding a bag of D-tagged chips, my inner monologue went full throttle: original price 12 yuan, now 3, two months of shelf life left. Buying it isn't spending. It's arbitrage.

An old gentleman next to me picked up a bag at full price, and I nearly tapped his shoulder: "Sir, you're overpaying, check the bottom-right corner... Never mind. Quietly making a fortune. Quietly making a fortune."

(Pause for laughter)

Honestly, the best part wasn't saving nine yuan. It was the smugness of asymmetric information. I finally understand why the Wall Street crowd is so obsessed with insider tips. This stuff is addictive.

---

The AC trick hits harder.

In summer, do you just hit "Cool"? Wrong. Try "Dry." In dehumidify mode the compressor runs intermittently, so the temperature drops slowly, but it feels about the same, and the power bill shrinks by nearly half.

The first time I tried it, that month's bill came in 80-odd yuan lighter. Staring at the bill, I sank into thought: all those summer nights, was my AC quietly mocking my ignorance in Cool mode the whole time?

Turns out I wasn't paying electricity bills. I was paying the idiot tax in installments.

Dry mode has a hidden bonus: it doesn't turn the room into a fridge. You don't wake up freezing at 3 a.m., you don't grope around for the remote, and you don't spend the night negotiating with your blanket, one foot in and one foot out, doing some form of thermal equilibrium yoga.

Scientists call that state the thermal neutral zone. Regular people call it "too hot under the blanket, too cold without it, so I just stick one knee out." Of everything the human body kept through evolution, the knee is the one part still assigned to temperature duty.

Dehumidify mode. Saving knees.

---

Phone batteries.

Do they feel less durable every year? Many people believe you should drain the battery before charging so it keeps its "memory." That's folklore from the nickel-metal hydride era. Modern lithium batteries fear nothing more than deep discharge and overcharging; the sweet spot is 20% to 80%.

Learning this was painful: for the past five years, I had been slowly murdering my phone battery.

I used to plug in before bed and charge all night, then walk out at 100% in the morning full of hope. In reality, once full, the phone sat there trickle-charging all night, the battery bouncing around in the heat, its lifespan draining fast. That isn't charging. That's torture.

So I changed my charging habits. I bought a timer plug that cuts power at 80%, and battery health now declines much more slowly.

Friends ask me: "Your phone is two years old. How is the battery still this healthy?"

I smile faintly: "Nothing special. I just... take good care of it."

The same face as when someone asks how my stocks made money and I answer "I read candlestick charts." Truth is, that's the only trick I know. But you can't lose on attitude.

---

Last one: refunds.

Ever bought a "non-refundable" flight or hotel? I used to accept my fate the moment I saw those words. Then I learned a move: wrong date or changed plans? Don't hit "refund." First change the booking to the cheapest date a few months out, then cancel. On many platforms, once a ticket has been changed, the refund rules are recalculated.

The first time it worked, more than 2,000 yuan came back. Staring at the refund notification, I felt half the joy of sudden wealth, half the grief of "so this is how much I'd been losing all along."

It's like finding out your ex is secretly a billionaire, except the entire time you were together, they insisted on splitting every bill.

No, wait, that metaphor doesn't quite work. But you know the feeling.

---

Looking back, these tricks are all one thing: information gaps. The rules sit right there. Most people don't know them, and past me kept quietly paying for not knowing.

I have a habit now: before buying anything, booking a hotel, or using an appliance, I spend five minutes searching "is there something about this I don't know." The return on those five minutes beats my mutual fund.

Seriously. My fund lost 15% last year, and the money these hacks saved has nearly filled the hole.

So if wealth management isn't going anywhere for you this year, don't sweat it. There's always life management.

The road to quietly striking it rich is actually pretty wide for ordinary folks.
